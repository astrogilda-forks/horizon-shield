Reading a contractor quote with public data

Toshikatsu Oga  /  September 27, 2026
  1. The guides
  2. How these guides are made

Each guide answers one question a homeowner asks while holding a quote, in the order the question comes up: what the public numbers are, how to test a quote against them, and what to do when the contractor's wording rather than the arithmetic is the problem. Every figure links to the government record it comes from (BLS wage statistics, Census import data, state DOT markup rules, City of Austin permit exemptions, the FTC Cooling-Off Rule, Texas Business and Commerce Code 27.02), and the wage, import, margin and markup figures are the same ones our reports print, published as USCCDB v1.0.

The guides say what public data can and cannot do. They give a floor for shingles and a reference for labor; they do not give a market price for a roof, and no government source does. Where a line has no public benchmark, the guides say so and tell you what to ask for instead.

The guides

Is my solar quote fair? Check the price per watt

Divide the cash price by the system size in watts DC. In 2025 the median price at each of the top 100 U.S. residential installers ranged from $2.4 to $6.3 per watt (Berkeley Lab). For systems you own, the federal 25D credit is not allowed when installation is completed after December 31, 2025, and solar loans can hide dealer fees of 10% to 30% of the cash price (CFPB).

HVAC replacement quote: what each line should say

Equipment, labor, materials, permit and disposal. Ask for the Manual J sizing behind the tons (IRC M1401.3), the model numbers and AHRI Certified Reference Number, and the crew hours; BLS national wages for HVAC installers are $29.33 median and $45.78 at the 90th percentile. The federal 25C credit ended for property placed in service after December 31, 2025.

How much does it cost to build a house? Read the bid per square foot

For contractor-built single-family houses started in 2025, the Census Bureau reports a median contract price of $404,000 and a median of $171.57 per square foot ($198.33 average), with land excluded. Regional medians ran from $157.30 (South) to $194.09 (Midwest). Divide your builder's bid by finished square feet, then check what sits inside it: site work, permits and fees, allowances and the builder's fee.

Roofing materials on a quote: what each line should say

Every materials line on a roofing quote should name a product and a quantity. Shingles and decking have a public floor from the Census import chain: $13.03 to $14.16 per 4 by 8 foot sheet of 7/16 inch OSB before freight and labor. Underlayment, ice barrier, drip edge, flashing and vents have no clean public price, but the 2021 IRC says where they go. Truss work may need a registered design professional; flat roof membranes have no clean public price benchmark.

Window repair or replacement: what the quote should say

A window quote either repairs parts of a window (glass, sash, hardware, weatherstripping) or replaces it (insert or full-frame), and it has to say which, window by window. Check the product against its NFRC label (U-factor 0.20 to 1.20, lower keeps more heat in). In pre-1978 homes, paid window replacement is never "minor repair" under EPA's lead rule (40 CFR 745.83), so the rule applies unless an exemption, such as a lead-free test result, covers the job. Glaziers earn $27.44 median and $43.17 at the 90th percentile per hour (BLS, May 2025). The federal 25C credit ended for property placed in service after December 31, 2025.

Water heater or boiler replacement quote: what each line should say

A water heater replacement quote is the unit, labor, code items, permit and haul-away. Check the model's UEF in the DOE Compliance Certification Database, labor against BLS plumbers' wages ($30.67 median and $52.13 at the 90th percentile per hour nationally, plain wages, not billing rates), and whether your city requires each add-on. The federal 25C credit ended for property placed in service after December 31, 2025.

Electrical panel upgrade quote: what to check before you sign

A panel or service upgrade quote should show a load calculation under National Electrical Code Article 220, the permit, the utility's disconnect, reconnect and meter steps, and each part as its own line. Electricians earn $30.38 median and $52.17 at the 90th percentile per hour, plain wages, not billing rates (BLS, May 2025). The federal 25C panel credit ended for property placed in service after December 31, 2025; DOE says state rebates are available in select states.

Bathroom exhaust fan install cost: what the quote should show

A bathroom exhaust fan quote is the fan, the duct and cap, the electrical work, the ceiling patch and the permit. The residential code lists 50 cfm intermittent or 20 cfm continuous for a bathroom and says the air goes outdoors, not into the attic. Check the fan's model against ENERGY STAR or HVI listings, the duct against the code's length table, and labor against BLS wages ($30.38 median, $52.17 at the 90th percentile for electricians nationally).

French drain or yard drainage quote: what it should specify

A French drain or yard drainage quote should state the linear feet, trench size, pipe, gravel, fabric and where the water ends up. Divide the price by the feet. Delaware's environmental agency put a contractor-installed 60-foot French drain at $1,000 to $4,000 (2022 dollars). 811 is free. Virginia law requires a locate request before excavation, except hand digging by the owner or occupant.

Home improvement loans: checking the financing that comes with a quote

When a contractor offers a loan with the quote, compare the cash price with the amount financed and the total of payments on the Truth in Lending disclosure. The CFPB found solar dealer fees typically run 10 to 30 percent of the cash price, added to the loan. A loan secured by your principal dwelling usually carries a right to rescind until midnight of the third business day (12 CFR 1026.23). A contractor may not take a credit contract, or accept the proceeds of a loan from a lender it refers you to, unless the contract carries the FTC Holder notice (16 CFR Part 433).

How much should a roof replacement cost in Austin, TX?

Public data do not give a market price for a roof. They give a shingle floor of about $82 to $89 per square and a loaded labor reference of $43.34 to $59.83 per crew hour in Austin, TX, with every source on the page.

Is my roofing quote too high?

You cannot tell from the total. Split the quote into labor, materials, permit, disposal and overhead, then check labor hours against BLS wages with a DOT loading and shingles against the import chain. Steps, formulas and sources.

The contractor says the price is only good today. What can I do?

For a sale made at your home over $25, the FTC Cooling-Off Rule (16 CFR Part 429) gives you until midnight of the third business day to cancel for a full refund, and the seller must hand you two copies of a cancellation form. What is covered, what is not, and how to cancel.

Can a roofer waive my insurance deductible in Texas?

No. Texas Business and Commerce Code Section 27.02 makes it a Class B misdemeanor for a contractor paid from a property insurance claim to pay, waive, absorb or rebate the deductible, and contracts of $1,000 or more must carry a printed notice. Insurance Code 4102.163 also bars roofers from adjusting the claim.

Do I need a permit to replace my roof in Austin, TX?

Often not. The City of Austin exempts asphalt shingles replacing asphalt shingles, and any roof covering replacement that does not affect the structure, on one and two family homes and townhouses up to three stories, unless the property is in the Wildland-Urban Interface area and 50% or more is replaced. Decking up to 128 square feet is exempt too.

How many squares is my roof?

A roofing square is 100 square feet of roof surface. Multiply the house footprint by the pitch factor (1.054 for 4/12, 1.118 for 6/12, 1.202 for 8/12, 1.302 for 10/12, 1.414 for 12/12) and divide by 100. A 2,000 square foot footprint at 6/12 is about 22.4 squares before waste.

What do construction trades earn per hour in Austin, TX?

BLS May 2025 wages for Austin, loaded with the TxDOT 1.80 factor: roofers $43.34 to $59.83, painters $40.66 to $51.89, carpenters $46.60 to $60.75, electricians $52.65 to $68.22, plumbers $55.17 to $70.02, HVAC $55.33 to $79.78 per crew hour (mean to 90th percentile).

Overhead and profit on a contractor quote: how much is normal?

State DOT rules put overhead and profit in the open: TxDOT allows 25% on labor plus 55% for insurance and taxes and 25% on materials; Caltrans 15% on materials; FDOT a single 17.5%. A separate 20% line on top of loaded labor recovers the same costs twice.

How much deposit should I pay a contractor?

The FTC says never pay the full amount up front. California caps the down payment on a home improvement contract at $1,000 or 10% of the price, whichever is less, and bars payments ahead of work done; Maryland caps the deposit at one third and bars any payment before the contract is signed. Texas sets no cap in the statutes cited here.

Does my contractor need EPA lead certification?

Yes, if the house was built before 1978 and the work disturbs more than 6 square feet of painted surface per interior room or 20 square feet outside, or replaces windows. The firm must be EPA certified, assign a certified renovator and give you the lead pamphlet before starting (40 CFR 745, Subpart E).

The contractor says material prices went up. Is the increase justified?

The BLS Producer Price Index for construction materials was 375.908 in August 2026 against 341.458 a year earlier, a rise of 10.1%. That justifies a higher materials line, not a higher total; on a quote where shingles are $4,420, 10.1% is about $446.

How do I get contractor quotes I can compare?

Three quotes for three different jobs cannot be compared. Write one scope of work (quantities, products, what is included and excluded, schedule, payment terms, permit, cleanup) and give every contractor the same page. The FTC advises multiple written estimates that describe the work, materials, completion date and price.

How these guides are made

Why public data instead of an AI answer: in a peer-reviewed study of 40 Japanese renovation price questions put to frontier AI models, the going-rate range the models stated was a median of 2.5 to 3 times wide, the price figures inside a single answer spanned a median of 10 times, and no answer offered a threshold for when a price becomes an over-charge without being asked (Oga, Intelligent Infrastructure and Construction 2(4):15, MDPI, 2026, doi:10.3390/iic2040015). These guides are the other half of that finding: a reference anyone can cite and recheck.

Every number is taken from a public source we read in the original, and the page links to it. Where we compute something, the formula is printed beside the result so you can redo it on a calculator. Statutes and city pages are quoted as published on the date shown at the top of each guide, with a link to the current text; we do not paraphrase a rule without linking it. The guides are written by Toshikatsu Oga, who spent thirty years in construction as a carpenter, site supervisor and construction manager before founding HORIZON SHIELD, and are reviewed against the same public sources our reports use.
